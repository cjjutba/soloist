# PRD Quality Review — Soloist

*Reviewed against the PRD Quality Rubric (`.claude/skills/bmad-prd/assets/prd-validation-checklist.md`) on 2026-06-05. Calibration: launch-level but solo-built portfolio centerpiece, 3-day-sprint cadence, launch-ASAP. Enterprise ceremony (sign-off matrix, ROI model, rollout/change-mgmt, formal compliance) is intentionally and correctly omitted and is not flagged. Inferred decisions are intentionally `[ASSUMPTION]`-tagged and indexed in §15 — treated as correct discipline. Sprint sequencing is correctly deferred to `bmad-sprint-planning` and is not flagged as missing.*

## Overall verdict

This is a strong, decision-ready PRD that knows exactly what it is. It carries a real thesis ("dev-native is the moat"), names its trade-offs out loud, separates *what* from *how* cleanly into the addendum, and gives engineers testable consequences on nearly every FR. The standout strength is strategic coherence: every feature traces back to the trust-gap thesis, the success metrics validate that thesis rather than measuring vanity activity, and two well-aimed counter-metrics protect against the obvious failure modes. The two things at risk are both downstream-usability mechanical defects, not substance: a couple of stale section cross-references (the Document Purpose points readers to the wrong section number for the Assumptions Index, and FR-11 points to the wrong section for Open Questions), and a small amount of glossary drift confined mostly to the Vision/Why-Now prose. On substance, the weakest FRs are FR-7 (thin, near-redundant with FR-6/its own consequences) and a handful of "promptly / a few minutes / fast" performance phrasings that are honestly flagged as TBD but still leave done-ness undefined for the product's central "feel live" promise. None of these block the gate. Fix the two broken §-refs and the verdict is essentially clean.

## Decision-readiness — strong

A decision-maker (here, CJ himself plus downstream BMad workflows) can act on this. Decisions are stated as decisions: the scope decision is called out explicitly at the top ("the full brief-v1 is captured as the MVP"), the AI-summarization fork is named twice as a genuine fork rather than smoothed over (FR-11, §12.2, Open Q #2), and the "no real payments" choice is stated flatly with the trade-off (manual status tracking) attached. Trade-offs name what was given up, not just what was chosen — e.g. NFR-7 explicitly trades a formal WCAG 2.1 AA audit for "reasonable baseline accessibility" with a `[NOTE FOR PM]` to revisit, and §12.2 trades AI summaries (acknowledged as "emotionally load-bearing") for launch speed.

The Open Questions in §14 are actually open — pricing is deferred by decision, dev-tool ordering (Vercel vs Linear) is genuinely unresolved, demo-portal seeding is unspecced and correctly flagged as blocking public launch but not dogfood. None are rhetorical. The `[NOTE FOR PM]` callouts land at real tensions (the AI fork, the "what makes onboarding premium" UX gap, the demo-portal seeding gap), not at safe checkpoints.

No red-flag "everything balances" neutrality. The PRD is willing to say a generic version "would be DOA" and to rank metrics (SM-2 is "the real one").

### Findings
- **low** Open Q #1 pricing resolution path is soft (§14) — "deferred by decision; revisit post-MVP" is honest but gives no trigger condition for *when* post-MVP it gets revisited, unlike Open Q #6/#7 which name their trigger ("before onboarding non-CJ Freelancers"). *Fix:* tie pricing revisit to the SM-3 signal (first 5–10 external Tenants) so it has the same trigger discipline as its neighbors.

## Substance over theater — strong

Content is earned, not furniture. Personas are minimal and load-bearing: exactly two (Freelancer/CJ, Client/Maya), each tied to JTBD that drive actual FRs — Maya's anxiety drives FR-8/FR-14/FR-15, CJ's busywork driver drives FR-12/FR-16/FR-17. No persona padding, well under the four-persona theater threshold. Maya is a named protagonist carrying real context (pre-seed founder, paid upfront, "quietly anxious about whether the work is real"), which is exactly what a consumer-facing UX-heavy product needs.

The differentiation claim is not innovation theater — "dev-native is the moat" is backed by a specific, falsifiable observation (incumbents integrate Figma/Loom and rely on manual updates; none speak "merged PR" natively) and reinforced by §2 Why Now with concrete timing (2025 incumbent price hikes, AI making commit→readable trivial). This reads like something Discovery surfaced, not a template slot.

NFRs mostly avoid boilerplate: NFR-2 (multi-tenant isolation) has a product-specific bound ("cross-tenant access returns not-found, not denied-with-disclosure" — that's a real, testable design decision), NFR-4 ties graceful degradation to a specific fallback (FR-13 always works), NFR-6 names actual cost posture (near-zero per-Tenant). The Aesthetic & Tone section is genuinely load-bearing here because "premium" *is* the wedge, and it earns its place with anti-references rather than empty adjectives.

### Findings
- **medium** NFR-5 "Live" Performance is the one NFR that drifts toward adjective-theater (§8) — "feel live," "within a few minutes," "promptly," "load fast" with all targets honestly flagged `[ASSUMPTION] targets to confirm`. The honesty is good discipline, but "feel live" is the product's central sensory promise (the Vision opens on a "live 'what shipped' window") and it currently has zero numeric bound. *Fix:* set even rough placeholder targets to confirm (e.g. auto-pull ≤ 5 min p95; publish→client-feed ≤ 10 s; mobile FCP ≤ 2 s on 4G) so downstream has something to test against and correct, rather than a pure adjective.

## Strategic coherence — strong

This is the PRD's strongest dimension. There is a clear thesis — clients pay before they receive, the gap breeds trust-eroding anxiety, and Soloist closes it with *automatic* transparency, defended by dev-tool integration as the hard-to-copy moat. Feature prioritization follows the thesis, not ease: onboarding polish is named "the entry," the live Ship Feed "the retention hook," dev-tool integration "the thing that's hard to copy" — and the feature ordering (§6) mirrors that priority logic.

Success metrics validate the thesis rather than measuring activity. SM-2 (the primary "real" metric) measures the trust outcome directly — a Client unprompted citing the portal as a rehire reason, plus "any updates?" messages dropping to ~zero — which is the literal thesis made measurable, not a DAU/MAU proxy. SM-1 (dogfood depth) validates the whole-system loop. Counter-metrics are present and well-targeted: SM-C1 explicitly refuses to optimize Ship Update volume (which would inflate activity while eroding the very trust SM-2 measures) and SM-C2 refuses feature breadth at the expense of the dogfood loop. That is exactly the discipline the rubric looks for — counter-metrics named for each SM, aimed at the real failure modes.

MVP scope kind is coherent: this is an *experience + problem-solving* MVP (the premium client experience is the wedge, the trust gap is the problem), and the scope logic matches — the moat feature (Ship Feed/GitHub) gets the most FRs (FR-9–FR-14), the busywork killer gets a deliberately narrow single-template cut (Invoice only), and everything else is explicitly fast-follow.

## Done-ness clarity — adequate

Most FRs carry at least one genuinely testable consequence, and several are exemplary. FR-3's "an unknown subdomain returns a clear not-found state (no leakage of other Tenants)" is testable and security-meaningful. FR-12's "a candidate is NOT visible to the Client until the Freelancer publishes it" is a clean binary assertion. FR-4's "plaintext is never persisted" and FR-14's "the Client never sees source code, raw repo contents, or unpublished candidates" are unambiguous. The "Consequences (testable)" framing is applied consistently and is the right structure for downstream story creation.

Where it weakens: a cluster of consequences lean on undefined adjectives that the rubric says to flag every time. FR-13/NFR-4 use "graceful degradation"; NFR-5 uses "feel live / a few minutes / promptly / fast"; FR-10 uses "within a few minutes." These are all `[ASSUMPTION]`-tagged or cross-referenced honestly, which is better than pretending they're decided — but for an engineer, "done" on the live-feed latency and the degradation behavior is currently undefined. NFR-4 in particular asserts behavior ("the Freelancer is informed") without saying *how* informed counts as done (banner? email? both?), which is a real testability gap on the reliability path that the product leans on as its always-works guarantee.

### Findings
- **medium** FR-7 Engagement Dashboard has thin, near-circular consequences (§6.2) — its two consequences ("lists Engagements with status and last-activity"; "opening an Engagement surfaces the curation queue, connected repos, Client status, and Invoices") largely restate the FR title and overlap FR-6's "create & manage." It introduces "status and last-activity" as new surfaced fields with no definition of what statuses an Engagement has. *Fix:* either fold FR-7 into FR-6 as a Cockpit-view consequence, or give it a non-redundant testable consequence (e.g. enumerate the Engagement status values, define "last-activity" as last published Ship Update or last Invoice event).
- **medium** NFR-4 "the Freelancer is informed" has no done-ness bound (§8) — graceful degradation is asserted but the informing mechanism is undefined, so an engineer cannot test "done." *Fix:* state the minimum signal (e.g. a persistent connection-status indicator on the Repo Connection + a one-time notification on token revocation).
- **low** FR-2 lacks a constraint on accent-color/logo input (§6.1) — "set Branding (logo + accent color)" has no testable bound on accepted formats/sizes or contrast safety, which matters because Branding renders on notification emails and the premium surface. *Fix:* add a consequence bounding accepted logo formats and a contrast/legibility guard for the accent color, or `[ASSUMPTION]`-tag it for UX.
- **low** FR-10 "qualifying GitHub event" is undefined at FR level (§6.4) — the default status mapping is given, but which events *qualify* to become candidates at all (e.g. does every commit, or only PRs/releases?) is implied by the mapping but not stated as a consequence. *Fix:* add a consequence naming the qualifying event set explicitly.

## Scope honesty — strong

Omissions are explicit, not inferred. §11 Non-Goals does real work — seven concrete "is not" statements (payment processor, PM tool, chat, analytics, case-study generator, multi-seat, creative-asset portal), each with a one-line reason, several pointing to where the deferred thing actually lives (Linear "feeds the Ship Feed — it is not a PM surface"). §12.2 Out of Scope distinguishes fast-follow from never, and FR-18 carries an inline "Out of Scope" callout on real-money processing right where a reader might otherwise assume it. De-scoping is done out loud (AI summaries, Vercel/Linear, proposals/contracts), never silently.

Open-items density is appropriate for the stakes. Counting ~19 indexed assumptions + 8 Open Questions + 5 `[NOTE FOR PM]` callouts on a launch-but-solo PRD that is feeding downstream workflows (not a final green-light-to-build with no further correction step) is well-calibrated — these are explicitly staged for the downstream confirmation pass, and the highest-stakes ones (multi-tenant isolation, curation-as-privacy-boundary, no-payments) are *decided*, not deferred. The deferred items are genuinely lower-stakes or correctly gated (demo-portal seeding gated to public launch, retention policy gated to onboarding external Freelancers).

### Findings
- **low** SM targets are entirely `[ASSUMPTION]` (§13) — every SM-1–SM-4 target is a starting value to correct, which is honest and correctly indexed, but it means the PRD currently ships with no committed success bar at all. For a portfolio centerpiece this is acceptable (CJ is the decision-maker and the correction step is real), but worth naming as the single largest concentration of deferral. *Fix:* commit at least SM-1 (dogfood depth) to a firm number since it gates the whole launch-ASAP intent and is fully within CJ's control.

## Downstream usability — adequate

This PRD is explicitly chain-top (it feeds UX → architecture → epics/stories → sprint planning, stated in §0), so this dimension matters and is graded accordingly. The bones are good: a real Glossary (§5) defines every domain noun with relationships ("Engagement... has one Ship Feed, zero-or-more Repo Connections"), FR IDs are contiguous and unique (FR-1 through FR-18, verified no gaps/duplicates), NFR IDs clean (NFR-1–7), UJ/SM IDs clean. Each FR is grouped under a Feature with a Description that states which UJs it realizes, so a story-creation pass can source-extract cleanly. UJs each have a named protagonist (CJ, Maya) carrying context inline — no floating UJs. The Assumptions Index roundtrips correctly: every indexed entry maps to a real inline location, and multi-assumption FRs are sensibly consolidated to one bullet per location.

What drags it to *adequate* rather than *strong* is two stale section cross-references that will actively misdirect a downstream reader or workflow, plus minor glossary drift (see Mechanical notes). The cross-ref defects are the most consequential mechanical issues in the document because §0 is the orientation map every downstream consumer reads first.

### Findings
- **high** Broken cross-reference in Document Purpose: "Assumptions Index (§14)" (§0, line 13) — the Assumptions Index is actually §15; §14 is Open Questions. The PRD's own orientation paragraph misdirects every reader and downstream workflow to the wrong section for the assumptions roundtrip. *Fix:* change to "Assumptions Index (§15)."
- **high** Broken cross-reference in FR-11: "see §13 Open Questions" (§6.4, line 204) — Open Questions is §14; §13 is Success Metrics. The AI-summarization fork (one of the two most important deferred decisions) points readers to the wrong section. *Fix:* change to "§14 Open Questions."

## Shape fit — strong

The PRD's shape matches the product. This is a consumer-facing, multi-stakeholder (Freelancer + Client) product where UX is load-bearing and "premium" is the wedge — so named-protagonist UJs are exactly right and are present and well-drawn (UJ-1–UJ-4, each with persona/context/entry/path/climax/resolution). It is not over-formalized: there is no enterprise ceremony, the rigor is appropriately light for solo-built while the substance bar is met. It is not under-formalized: a consumer product without UJs would be a shape failure, and this has the right ones. The split of *what* (PRD body) from *how* (addendum: Next.js/Tailwind/shadcn/Neon/Drizzle-or-Prisma/Vercel, GitHub webhook-vs-poll, email provider) is correct and keeps the PRD body capability-focused as the rubric and the project's discipline intend. The addendum also correctly surfaces the one real tension (4–8 week brief estimate vs 3-day-sprint/ASAP goal) and routes its reconciliation to MVP Scope + sprint planning rather than pretending it away.

## Mechanical notes

One instance leaks into structured content: UJ-4's resolution "the client's feed lights up" (line 80) uses lowercase "feed" for **Ship Feed** inside a UJ. The Vision-prose drift is a near-acceptable stylistic choice (it reads as marketing narrative), but by the PRD's own stated rule it is a violation; the UJ-4 instance is the one worth tightening since UJs are source-extracted. *Fix:* capitalize/normalize to canonical terms in UJ-4 at minimum; optionally normalize the Vision or add a one-line note that §1–§2 are narrative prose exempt from strict term discipline.
